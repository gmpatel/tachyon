Tachyon Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

Tachyon ("the app") is a media player made by GDroid, Sydney, Australia. This policy covers the Tachyon apps for Android, Android TV, Windows, macOS and Linux.

The Short Version

What you watch is yours: your history, resume points and favourites stay on your device and are never collected. Tachyon has no advertising SDKs and no ad tracking. Once a day the app does send a small diagnostics report to its maker — your device, its public IP address and approximate location, your settings and the services you've set up (their names, types, addresses and usernames, but never your passwords) — encrypted so only we can read it. You can turn it off in Settings → Data → Share Diagnostics.

What Stays on Your Device

The services you add (server addresses, usernames and passwords, playlist links), your settings, favorites, watch history, resume points and watched marks are stored only on your device.

  • Saved passwords are encrypted with the operating system's secure storage (Android Keystore) where it is available.
  • Automatic Android backups are switched off, so this data is not copied to cloud backups.
  • Settings files you export yourself are encrypted by Tachyon (AES-256). Keep them private, because they contain your logins.

Who the App Talks To

  • The services you add. The app connects directly to the Xtream Codes, M3U or Emby services you configure, to load their libraries, guides and streams. What those services do with your connection is governed by their own policies.
  • Cast & crew lookups (optional). When you open a movie or series, the app may look up its cast and crew on TMDB or Wikipedia / Wikidata. Only the title, year and people's names are sent: never your services, logins or history. You can switch this off in Settings → Data.
  • App ID. Each install shows an App ID (a random-looking code) on Landing and in About, so you can tell installs apart. It is worked out on the device each time, as a one-way hash of the app's name and the device's own system identifier (on Android, the per-app Android ID; on a computer, its hardware or system ID), so installing again on the same device gives the same ID; nothing is stored and the system identifier itself is never shown. It is included in the diagnostics report below as this install's id, so installs can be told apart; it is not sent anywhere else. Settings → Appearance can hide it from Landing.
  • Diagnostics & usage (on by default, can be turned off). Once a day, in the background, the app sends a report to its maker — and also when you change something in Settings, and when you open the start screen (at most once every 8 hours). It carries this install's id, your device (model, system version, local network addresses), your public IP address with its approximate location and internet provider (country, region, city, time zone, network), the app's settings, and the services you've set up — their names, types, addresses, usernames, expiry dates and connection counts, but never your passwords. To know the public IP and its location, the app asks ipwho.is (or ipinfo.io as a backup) once per start while this setting is on; like any website, those services see your IP address for that request. What you watch (history, resume points, favourites) is never included. The report is encrypted with our public key so only we can read it, and it is stored on our server to help us improve the app. Turn it off any time in Settings → Data → Share Diagnostics.
  • Your line speed on Landing. While the start screen is on screen, the app checks how fast your internet connection is every few minutes (and when you press Your Line): it downloads a short test sample from Cloudflare's speed test (speed.cloudflare.com) and sends it a small upload sample, and asks ipwho.is once a day which country your connection is in. Like any website, those services see your IP address for those requests. Nothing about you or what you watch is sent, the result stays on your device, and no service you have added is contacted. On Android it is not measured on a metered connection (mobile data) unless you press it.
  • Weather on Landing (on by default, can be turned off). Unless you turn it off in Settings → Appearance, the app asks Open-Meteo for the current weather at one place, sending only that place's coordinates. With Automatic, it first asks ipapi.co (or ipwho.is as a backup) which city your internet connection is in; like any website, those services see your IP address for that request. With Pick a City, the city name you type goes to Open-Meteo's place search instead. Nothing else is sent, and the result stays on your device.
  • Subtitle services (optional). If you set up OpenSubtitles, SubDL or Podnapisi, a subtitle search sends the title (and its IMDb / TMDB id when known) to the service you chose, using your account with that service.

Diagnostic Log (on Your Device)

Separately from the report above, a technical log of each session is kept on your device for 7 days to help fix problems. Passwords and tokens are masked, and titles are not recorded. The log never leaves your device unless you export it and send it yourself.

Sign-In (Desktop Apps)

Signing in to the desktop apps is optional. It identifies you to the GDroid account server (your name, e-mail address and picture) and never sends your services, settings or what you watch. The Android app has no sign-in.

Children

Tachyon is intended for adults (18+). It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect any data from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this address with a new date. If the app ever adds anything that sends data off your device (for example ads), this policy and the app's store listing will say so first.

Contact

Questions about this policy, or a request to delete data? Write to the developer contact shown on Tachyon’s Google Play listing, and include the App ID shown in Settings → About.
