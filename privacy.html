Tachyon Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 October 2026

Tachyon ("the app") is a free media player made and maintained by one person: an independent developer who publishes as GDroid, in Sydney, Australia ("the developer", "we", "us"). It is freeware: there is no company behind it, nothing to buy and no advertising. This policy covers the Tachyon apps for Android, Android TV / Google TV / Fire TV, Windows, macOS and Linux, and the Tachyon website.

The Short Version

What you watch is yours: your history, resume points and favourites stay on your device and are never collected. Tachyon has no advertising SDKs and no ad tracking, and it doesn't sell or share your data with anyone. Once a day the app does send a small diagnostics report to the developer — your device, its public IP address and approximate location, your settings and the services you've set up (their names, types, addresses and usernames, but never your passwords) — encrypted so only we can read it. You can turn it off in Settings → Data → Share Diagnostics.

Tachyon Is Only a Player

Tachyon doesn't provide, host, sell, recommend or check any channels, films or other content. You add your own services (an Xtream Codes account, an M3U playlist, an Emby server, or a free list from How To → Try Open Live TV), and the app plays what those services send. You are responsible for making sure you're allowed to use the services and content you add, and for following the laws where you live and the terms of the services you use. The free lists offered in the app are examples kept by independent projects; the developer doesn't control them and isn't responsible for what they contain.

What Stays on Your Device

The services you add (server addresses, usernames and passwords, playlist links), your settings, favorites, watch history, resume points and watched marks are stored only on your device.

  • Saved passwords are encrypted with the operating system's secure storage (Android Keystore) where it is available.
  • Automatic Android backups are switched off, so this data is not copied to cloud backups.
  • Settings files you export yourself are encrypted by Tachyon (AES-256). Keep them private, because they contain your logins.
  • The Owner / Admin key, App Lock PIN and similar codes are checked on the device and kept there in a protected form; they are never sent anywhere.

Who the App Talks To

  • The services you add. The app connects directly to the Xtream Codes, M3U or Emby services you configure, to load their libraries, guides and streams. What those services do with your connection is governed by their own policies.
  • Cast & crew lookups (optional). When you open a movie or series, the app may look up its cast and crew on TMDB or Wikipedia / Wikidata. Only the title, year and people's names are sent: never your services, logins or history. You can switch this off in Settings → Data.
  • App ID. Each install shows an App ID (a random-looking code) on Landing and in About, so you can tell installs apart. It is worked out on the device each time, as a one-way hash of the app's name and the device's own system identifier (on Android, the per-app Android ID; on a computer, its hardware or system ID), so installing again on the same device gives the same ID; nothing is stored and the system identifier itself is never shown. It is included in the diagnostics report below as this install's id, so installs can be told apart; it is not sent anywhere else. Settings → Appearance can hide it from Landing.
  • Diagnostics & usage (on by default, can be turned off). Once a day, in the background, the app sends a report to the developer — and also when you change something in Settings, and when you open the start screen (at most once every 8 hours). It carries this install's id, your device (model, system version, local network addresses), your public IP address with its approximate location and internet provider (country, region, city, time zone, network), the app's settings, and the services you've set up — their names, types, addresses, usernames, expiry dates and connection counts, but never your passwords. To know the public IP and its location, the app asks ipwho.is (or ipinfo.io as a backup) once per start while this setting is on; like any website, those services see your IP address for that request. What you watch (history, resume points, favourites) is never included. The report is encrypted with our public key so only we can read it, and it is stored on a server the developer runs, to help improve the app. Turn it off any time in Settings → Data → Share Diagnostics.
  • Crash reports (with Share Diagnostics on, Android). If the app closes unexpectedly, the next start sends one technical report about it to the same server: what went wrong in the app's code, the device's memory and system details, and the app's own recent log lines. It holds no titles, links, logins or passwords, and it is sent once.
  • Update checks. Now and then (at most every 12 hours, when you open the app) it asks the Tachyon website (gmpatel.github.io, hosted by GitHub) and, on Android, the Google Play listing whether a newer version exists. Updating downloads the new version from the developer's Dropbox or Google Drive. Like any website, those services see your IP address for those requests; nothing about you is sent.
  • Your line speed on Landing. While the start screen is on screen, the app checks how fast your internet connection is every few minutes (and when you press Your Line): it downloads a short test sample from Cloudflare's speed test (speed.cloudflare.com) and sends it a small upload sample, and asks ipwho.is once a day which country your connection is in. Like any website, those services see your IP address for those requests. Nothing about you or what you watch is sent, the result stays on your device, and no service you have added is contacted. On Android it is not measured on a metered connection (mobile data) unless you press it.
  • Weather on Landing (on by default, can be turned off). Unless you turn it off in Settings → Appearance, the app asks Open-Meteo for the current weather at one place, sending only that place's coordinates. With Automatic, it first asks ipapi.co (or ipwho.is as a backup) which city your internet connection is in; like any website, those services see your IP address for that request. With Pick a City, the city name you type goes to Open-Meteo's place search instead. Nothing else is sent, and the result stays on your device.
  • How To. To show the lists that suit you (Australian live TV, Try Open Live TV), How To asks ipapi.co (or ipwho.is) which country and region your connection is in. A list you add is then loaded from the address it came with (for example GitHub or i.mjh.nz), like any service you add yourself.
  • Subtitle services (optional). If you set up OpenSubtitles, SubDL or Podnapisi, a subtitle search sends the title (and its IMDb / TMDB id when known) to the service you chose, using your account with that service.
  • Casting (phones and computers). When you cast to a Chromecast or Google TV, the app uses Google's Cast technology to find TVs on your home network and hands the TV the stream's address to play. That TV then connects to your service itself.
  • Easy Settings (TV app). While the TV app shows its About page, it opens a page on your home network so a phone or computer on the same Wi-Fi can change its settings. It answers only devices on your home network that have the day's code, and it stops when you leave the page. Its QR code opens a small page on the Tachyon website that passes the TV's home-network address to your phone's browser; that address stays on your phone (it is never sent to the website).

Diagnostic Log (on Your Device)

Separately from the reports above, a technical log of each session is kept on your device for 7 days to help fix problems. Passwords and tokens are masked, and titles are not recorded. The log never leaves your device unless you export it and send it yourself.

Sign-In (Desktop Apps)

Signing in to the desktop apps is optional. It identifies you to the GDroid account server (your name, e-mail address and picture) and never sends your services, settings or what you watch. The Android app has no sign-in.

How Long We Keep Data, and How It's Protected

Diagnostics and crash reports are kept only as long as they help: the server keeps the latest ones for each install and deletes older ones (at the latest after about 90 days, apart from a small number of the newest per install). They are encrypted in transit and readable only by the developer. We take reasonable care to protect them, but no system connected to the internet can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

Your Choices

  • Turn off Share Diagnostics (Settings → Data) to stop the reports and crash reports; turn off the weather, cast & crew lookups and other optional features where they are offered.
  • Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data, removes everything it kept on your device.
  • To ask what diagnostics we hold for your install, or to have them deleted, contact us with the App ID shown in About.

Children

Tachyon is intended for adults (18+). It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect any data from anyone under 18. If you believe a child's data has reached us, contact us and we'll delete it.

Changes, and New Features

Tachyon keeps improving, and future versions may add features — for example optional accounts, syncing between your devices, richer diagnostics or new online services. Some of them may need to use more or different information to work. When a new feature uses your data in a way this policy doesn't already describe, we'll update this policy before or when that version is released, describe what it uses and why, keep it to what the feature needs, and where the law requires, make it optional or ask for your consent first. If the app ever adds advertising, this policy and the app's store listing will say so first. The current version always lives at this address with its date; using a version of the app released after a change means that version's features are covered by the updated policy.

Contact

Questions about this policy, or a request to delete data? Write to the developer contact shown on Tachyon’s Google Play listing, and include the App ID shown in Settings → About.
