Getting Started

Add your services, pick what to watch and find your way around.

Welcome to Tachyon

A fast, private player for your Xtream Codes, M3U and Emby services, on desktop, Android phones and Android TV.

Tachyon plays the live TV, movies, series and music of the services you add. It doesn’t come with any channels or content of its own: it is a player, like a TV set, for subscriptions and servers you already have.

  • Every service in one place. Add as many Xtream Codes accounts, M3U playlists and Emby servers as you like, switch between them, or watch them all merged.
  • One connection per service. Tachyon never opens a second stream to the same account, so shared and single-connection plans keep working. See One Connection Per Service.
  • Self-healing playback. Stalls, frozen pictures, broken clocks and dropped connections are noticed and fixed while you watch.
  • Built for every screen. Mouse and keyboard on a computer, touch on a phone, and the D-pad of a TV remote.

Adding a Service

Connect an Xtream Codes account, an M3U playlist or an Emby server.

Open Settings → Services and choose Add Service (on Android also from the navigation drawer’s Manage Services). Pick the kind of service at the top of the form.

Xtream Codes

  • Server URL with its port, for example http://example.com:8080, plus the Username and Password from your provider. A new form starts the address as http://:8080 (Emby: http://:8096) with the cursor where the server name goes, so on a TV remote you only type the host.
  • Test Connection checks the login and shows the account: status, expiry date, trial, and how many connections the plan allows.
  • Tachyon reads live channels, movies, series, the TV guide and catch-up through the provider’s Xtream API (player_api.php and xmltv.php).

M3U Playlist

  • Playlist URL: an http(s) link to an .m3u / .m3u8 playlist.
  • Or fill in the panel login above it — Server URL, Username and Password — and Tachyon builds the playlist link (get.php) and the TV guide link (xmltv.php) for you. Saved that way it is shown as M3U Panel, with Xtream catch-up.
  • The TV guides named in the playlist header (url-tvg, x-tvg-url) are used automatically; you can add your own under Advanced → TV Guides.
  • Catch-up, per-channel request headers and channel groups are read from the playlist. Details for providers are in M3U Tags Tachyon Reads.

Xtream Codes and M3U, Either Way

Many providers offer both an Xtream login and a get.php playlist link for the same account. Tachyon handles both:

  • Pasting a get.php link as a playlist offers Add As Xtream Codes (shown as M3U (Xtream Codes)): you get the full Xtream library with series information.
  • On an Xtream login, Add As M3U Playlist loads the channel list from the provider’s get.php playlist and the guide from xmltv.php instead (shown as M3U Panel).
  • A saved service can be switched later with Switch to M3U / Switch to Xtream Codes in its form. Favorites, history and watched marks stay with their titles.

Emby Server

  • Server URL including the port, for example http://192.168.1.20:8096, and your Emby Username and Password (leave the password empty if the user has none).
  • Test Connection shows the server’s name and version.
  • Live TV, movie and TV show libraries, music libraries and the server’s TV guide all come in. See Emby Servers.

Landing, All Services and Switching

Choose one service or merge them all, and switch whenever you like.

The Landing screen (“What are we watching?”) shows a tile for each service and, when you have two or more, All Services, which merges every enabled service into one library: one Home, one channel list, one guide.

  • Phones The navigation drawer (menu button, top left) lists every service, All Services, and Landing Screen.
  • Android TV The service switcher sits near the bottom of the left rail; it opens the list beside the rail.
  • Desktop Open Landing or switch services from the rail on the left: Landing Screen, then the service right under it.
  • Behind the tiles a speed-themed scene plays, a new one every few loops. The dots at the top pick one; the last dot turns the animations off (and back on).
  • Desktop Lens, the tile after Settings, opens Tachyon Lens: pick an HDMI capture card or a camera and the window becomes just the picture. Stop brings back its sources; Back (or Esc) there returns to Landing.
  • Desktop Your Line under the clock is the speed of your internet connection, checked every few minutes while Landing is open (Cloudflare’s speed test; no service you added is contacted). Click it to check again now.
  • Open your last pick straight away next time on Landing skips the picker on the next start.
  • Settings → Services → Startup: Ask Which Service to Watch at Startup. Off restores your last choice.

Updates

  • On Landing, over “What Are We Watching?”, a small New Release tag with a rocket and the new version shows when a newer Tachyon is out; when you have the newest, a check with a moon in orbit says Latest.
  • Tachyon checks Google Play and the Tachyon website when it starts or you come back to it, at most every 12 hours. The newer of the two counts.
  • Select Latest to check right away: a newer release turns it into New Release; otherwise a message says you have the newest.
  • Select New Release to see both versions, What’s New, and how to update for the way you installed Tachyon: Google Play, Downloader, or an APK file.
  • Installed from a Downloader code, an APK or a download on a computer? Select Update Now there: Tachyon downloads the new version from the developer’s signed source, checks the signature, installs it over your copy and restarts (Android opens its installer — select Update / Install). Your services, favorites and settings stay. Google Play updates its own copies.

Order and Tags

  • Drag the service cards in Settings → Services, or use their arrows, to set the order. Landing and the service menu follow it.
  • Every card, channel and row carries a small service tag in the service’s colour, merged or not.
  • Favorites follow your pick: with one service selected you see its favorites; All Services combines them.
A service card in Settings shows its account live: status, expiry and connections in use. Tachyon also warns once per session when a subscription expires within three days.

The Demo Service

GDroid Fake: an offline service to explore every feature without an account.

GDroid Fake is built into the app. It has 24 live channels with a two-day TV guide, 30 movies, 10 series with episodes and a small music library of 8 albums, all with generated artwork. Nothing goes over the network.

  • Live channels loop short test-signal clips (old TV looks: bars, test cards, snow); movies and episodes are three-minute clips, so resume, Continue Watching and Next Up work.
  • Every clip is captioned “GDroid Fake · Demo Preview · Not Real Content”.
  • A fresh install adds it once. Remove it like any other service and it stays removed; add it back with Just Exploring? Try the Demo on the Welcome screen or the Demo choice in the service form.

Signing In (Optional)

Tachyon works fully without an account; a GDroid sign-in only adds your name and avatar.

You never need an account to use Tachyon. Signing in with a GDroid account shows your name and avatar in the app and reveals the developer’s contact details in About.

  • Phones Sign-in opens in a Chrome Custom Tab.
  • Android TV TVs have no full browser, so sign-in opens in an in-app view.
  • Desktop Sign-in opens in your default browser and comes back to the app by itself.
  • Your sign-in is stored encrypted on the device (the system keychain, or the Android Keystore) and is never included in settings exports.

Home, Live TV, the guide, movies, series, music, search and favorites.

Your rows: continue where you left off, the next episodes, what’s on now and what’s new.

Home gathers the rows below. Rows with nothing to show stay hidden. Choose which rows appear, and their order, in Settings → Appearance → Home Rows.

The spotlight at the top shows the card you point at Desktop or move to Android TV: its title, year, genre, rating and service, over its picture. The clock and the weather sit at its right. Under it, Browse opens Live TV, Movies, Series, the TV Guide, Music and Favorites in one click.

Card Actions

  • Open a card’s menu with a right-click, a long press or its ⋯ button Desktop, a long press Phones or by holding OK Android TV. Every card has the same menu: Remove (on a Continue Watching, Next Up or Recently Watched row), Mark as Watched, Favorites, Go to Details and Resume Playing (or Play); a channel has TV Guide and Watch Fullscreen.
  • On Continue Watching and Next Up cards, I opens the details and Delete removes the card Desktop.
  • Channel rows carry ♥, TV Guide, Cast (phones and desktop) and ▶, and a recently watched one a ✕. TV Move → onto them; ↓ keeps the same button on the next row.
  • Clear, next to the Continue Watching and Recently Watched titles (Home, Movies, Series and Live TV), empties that section after asking. Watched marks and favorites stay.

Live TV

Categories on the left, channels in the middle, a live preview on the right.

The Category Rail

  • Every category gets a matching icon; pinned categories sit on top and your Favorites category is always there.
  • Categories are listed A–Z by default (symbols like “*” or “|” don’t count, and “Sport 2” comes before “Sport 10”); switch to Z–A or Provider Order under the category search. Last Used keeps the five categories you picked last, above the list.
  • Browse By groups categories by what the provider’s names say: Platforms, Channels & Networks, Languages, Genres, Quality, Years, Collections and Regions. It appears only when the names really are organised that way.
  • “GROUP | SUB” categories fold under their group. With All Services, each service has its own foldable header.
  • From a category’s menu: Pin to Top or Hide Category. Manage both in Settings → Content & Parental.
  • Each service remembers the category you were on — Live TV, Movies, Series and the TV Guide each keep their own. Switching to another service shows that service’s last category (or All); All Services keeps the category you came from.

Channels and the Preview

  • Selecting a channel plays it in the preview pane; a double-click or Watch Fullscreen goes full screen on the same stream, without reconnecting. Turn previews off with Preview Channels When Selected in Settings → Playback.
  • The preview shows what’s on now and Up Next; the bell next to an upcoming programme sets a reminder.
  • A channel with catch-up shows its archive window (for example “Catch-up: 7 days”).
  • Other Versions of This Channel lists the 4K / FHD / HD / SD copies of the same channel.
  • Channel numbers show when Show Channel Numbers is on (Settings → Appearance).
  • The bar over the list sorts the channels: Provider (the service’s own order), Number, A–Z and Added. Select the lit switch again to reverse it. Long lists come in pages of 100, with the pager under the sort bar (Pages lists every page) and Next Page at the end of each page (TV ↓ past the last channel). Zapping in the player still runs through the whole list.

Zapping

In the full-screen player, channel up / down moves through the list you opened the channel from: a category, Favorites or search results. See The Player.

TV Guide

A timeline of every channel, with programme filters, reminders, Start Over and catch-up.

  • The grid shows each channel’s programmes on a timeline, with the programmes on now highlighted. The time bar on top moves it: Now jumps back to the present, the arrows an hour and a half earlier or later.
  • Filters opens the Guide Filters: Show (All Programmes, Movies, Sports, Kids, News, Series: only those the guide really has) dims what doesn’t match and hides channels with nothing matching; your choice is remembered. Below it, the channel categories, as on Live TV.
  • Select a programme to see its details: Watch a programme on now, Remind Me for one coming up, and Start Over or Watch from Archive where the channel has catch-up.
  • Reload Guide (in Filters) downloads the guide again; otherwise it refreshes on its own (every 8 hours by default, Settings → Data & Storage). Only Channels With Guide Data there hides the channels the guide doesn’t list.
  • Android TV The Guide opens from the TV Guide button at the top of Live TV, or from the TV Guide button in the player’s bottom bar — that one opens it on the channel you were watching.

Where Guide Data Comes From

  • Xtream Codes: the panel’s xmltv.php, plus a short per-channel guide when you open a channel.
  • M3U: the guides in the playlist header and any you add yourself.
  • Emby: the server’s own guide.
  • Programme times wrong by an hour or more? Use Guide Time Offset in the service’s Advanced options.

Catch-Up, Start Over and Reminders

Watch programmes that already aired, restart the current one, and get a nudge before a show starts.

Catch-Up

When the provider keeps an archive for a channel, past programmes in the guide can be played. The archive window comes from the provider (Xtream) or the playlist (M3U); Archive Days in the service’s Advanced options can override it.

Start Over and Back to Live

  • Start Over plays the current programme from its beginning. It’s in the guide’s programme details, the player’s guide panel and the player controls; Android in the player’s options (hold OK).
  • You can seek within a catch-up programme like a recording.
  • Back to Live (or G Desktop; Android in the player’s options) returns to the live channel.

Reminders

  • Remind Me on an upcoming programme (in the guide, the Live preview’s Up Next, or Search’s Coming Up) sets a reminder; a bell marks it.
  • About a minute before the start, a notice offers Watch Now. Tachyon never switches channels by itself.
  • Reminders are saved with your settings and travel with an export.

Movies & Series

Browse, resume, mark as watched, and pick audio, subtitles and versions before you press Play.

  • Movies and Series have their own grids with the same category rail and Browse By groups as Live TV.
  • The bar over the grid sorts it: Provider (the service’s own order), Added, A–Z, Year and Rating. Select the lit switch again to reverse it (A–Z becomes Z–A, newest year first becomes oldest). Titles without a year or rating go last. Each page remembers its order.
  • Search in This List (the search button beside Filters on Android) narrows the page to titles with your words anywhere in the name, or the year: *matrix 1999*. The sort and the pages then work on what it found. Your last 10 searches are kept for each service and page and offered when the search opens, newest first.
  • Big libraries come in pages of 100. The pager under the sort bar has first, previous, the page numbers around the one you are on, next and last; Pages lists every page with its titles, to jump straight to one. The end of a page says what comes next, with Next Page. TV Press ↓ past the last row for the next page.
  • A title’s page shows its artwork, then Play (or Resume), Start Over, Trailer when there is one, and Shuffle for a series.
  • The story shows in two lines; select it to read all of it. The format (container, video, resolution, audio) follows it.
  • Cast & Crew shows the people with their photos. Select one for their biography and More With — their other titles in the service you are browsing (all your services with All Services). Lookups: Settings → Data.
  • The icon buttons add a favorite, mark as watched, open the title in an external player Desktop and copy the stream link.
  • Version, Audio and Subtitles selectors let you choose before playing, where the source has choices. The title remembers what you chose.
  • Subtitles opens a box: the source’s own subtitles first, then — when a subtitle service is set up in Settings → Subtitles — the matches it finds, each marked Downloaded or Not Downloaded. Select one that isn’t downloaded yet and it downloads, then is selected. Downloaded subtitles are kept for 90 days.
  • Available In lists the other versions of the title — the same TMDB id (or, without one, the same poster) in other languages, cuts or services (Telugu, Hindi, …); select one to switch to it. Lists show such a title once (Group Versions of a Title).

Watched, Resume and Next Up

  • Titles count as watched at 90 %. A ✓ marks watched titles; shows show how many episodes are left.
  • Mark as Watched / Unwatched works on a title, an episode or a whole season, from the menu or the ✓ button.
  • Movies and episodes resume where you stopped (Resume Movies & Episodes Where You Left Off in Settings → Playback). Resume also brings back the audio and subtitles that were playing when you stopped; Play from the beginning uses what the title’s page has selected.
  • The next episode can start by itself (Autoplay Next Episode), with a notice to cancel it.
  • Every title page ends with More Like This.
Hide Spoilers (Settings → Content & Parental) blurs the pictures and hides the story of episodes you haven’t watched yet.

Emby Extras

With Emby, watched marks, favorites, resume points and Next Up are the server’s own and stay in sync both ways. Intro markers allow Skip Intro, and the seek bar can show thumbnails. See Emby Servers.

Music

Albums, artists, songs and genres from Emby music libraries, with a music bar that follows you.

The Music section appears only when a service has music: an Emby music library, or the demo.

  • Albums / Artists / Songs / Genres, album pages with the track list by disc, Play / Shuffle / Add to Queue, and artist pages with Top Songs.
  • The music bar stays at the bottom while you browse; it opens Now Playing with shuffle, repeat and a queue you can reorder.
  • Android Music keeps playing with the screen off or in the background, with lock-screen, notification and headset controls.
  • Desktop Media keys and the system media overlay work.
  • Songs follow the one-connection rule: a song stops that service’s video first, and a video stops its music.

Search

Find channels, movies, series and programmes, by typing or by voice.

  • Phones Tap the search bar at the top; it opens a full-screen search. The mic starts voice search when the device has a speech recogniser.
  • Android TV Search is the first item of the left rail. The remote’s Search or mic button starts voice search directly.
  • Desktop Press Ctrl K or / anywhere.
  • Results cover channels, movies and series of the service in view (or All Services), and from three letters on, guide programmes On Now (plays the channel) and Coming Up in the next 24 hours (sets a reminder).
  • While Hide Adult Categories is on, adult categories stay out of search too.

Favorites

Keep channels, titles and music one press away, in your own order.

  • Tap ♥ on a channel or poster, press * on the focused item Desktop, or use Add to Favorites in its menu (hold OK on a TV).
  • The Favorites page shows everything; its Filters pick one kind (Channels, Movies, Series, Music). Live TV and the guide have a Favorites category too.
  • Favorites → Channels → Reorder sets your own channel order (on a TV: OK picks a channel up, ↑ ↓ move it, OK drops it). Home, Live TV and the guide follow it.
  • With Emby, favorites sync with the server.

Desktop, Phone and TV Navigation

Desktop

Android TV and Google TV

  • Android TV In Settings, hold OK anywhere (or press Menu) for the list of sections, and Back from deep in a page brings you up to the top first.
  • Text fields open the on-screen keyboard only after OK; Back ends typing.

  • Tachyon’s Continue Watching also appears in the Google TV Continue watching row.

Playing and Zapping

The controls, the channel list and guide panels, and changing channels.

The Controls

On a computer, then the channel / episode list, VLC / mpv, the mini player (P) and full screen (F).

Changing Channels

  • Desktop PgUp / PgDn change channel at once (a card shows the number, logo, name and what’s on); type a number to jump to it (Enter switches at once); Q goes back to the previous channel.
  • With the controls hidden, ↑ ↓ open the Channels list beside the picture: two channels before and after the one picked, the one playing marked ● Playing. ↓ moves to the next, ↑ to the previous, OK plays the picked one; Back, or 5 s without a key, closes it and the channel keeps playing. Stream Tags and the Info Banner

What the small tags on the channel banner, next to the playing time and in Stats for Nerds say about a stream.

Where They Show

  • The player’s header, under the Stream Info line: one row of small tags — the picture’s (resolution, codec, HDR, frame rate), the sound’s (codec, channels, language) and the stream’s (container, decoder, what the player does) — while the controls are up and for a few seconds after a channel starts.
  • Desktop Stats for Nerds (I) shows the tags too, with the delivery, the path, the index, the provider’s answer and the title’s bitrate.
  • Point at any tag to see what it means.
A tag only says what the stream itself reported. If something isn’t known, no tag is shown. For example, a picture whose scan type wasn’t reported shows FHD, not a guessed 1080p.

Index First or Index At End

A movie file holds an index: where every frame sits in the file. An MP4 keeps it in its moov box, a Matroska file in its Cues. The player needs it before it can show the first picture of an MP4, and before any seek.

  • Index First (“faststart”): the index arrives with the first bytes, so the title starts on a single read.
  • Index At End: the player reads the start, then jumps to the end for the index, then comes back. On a slow provider every jump waits for its first byte, and a 2-hour film’s index can be several MB, so start-up takes longer.
  • Tachyon keeps each title’s head and index on the device (Movie Index Cache). The next start of that title, even after a restart, skips the wait and shows Index Cached. On Xtream and M3U services, a movie’s or series’ details page left open a moment reads it ahead of time too — only while nothing plays and the account has a free connection, and Play stops it at once.

One Connection Per Service

Why Tachyon never opens a second stream to the same account, and what that means for you.

Many IPTV plans allow one stream at a time, and some are shared by a group (for example 8 people sharing an 8-connection plan). A second connection, even for a moment, takes someone else’s slot or trips the provider’s limit. So Tachyon holds at most one stream connection per provider account, at any moment.

How It Keeps to One

  • One player: the Live TV preview and full screen are the same stream, and the mini player too.
  • Changing channel closes the old connection, waits a short gap, then opens the new one. Rapid presses open only the channel you stop on.
  • Retries never overlap, and Tachyon never “probes” stream links to test them.
  • The same account added twice (an Xtream login and its M3U link, say) shares the one connection.
  • Music and video of the same service take turns: a song stops that service’s video, and the other way round.
  • Desktop An external player gets the stream only after Tachyon closed its own; the FFmpeg converter reads through the same single connection.
  • A paused live stream gives its slot back after a while (Android: 60 s for live, 5 min for movies), and so do going to the background, sleep, a crash or quitting.
  • Only one copy of Tachyon runs per device.

What It Means for You

  • Watching on two devices at once needs a plan with two connections; each device uses one.
  • Browsing, the guide, account details and subtitles are API requests, not streams; they don’t use a slot.
  • With a one-connection account, “The provider refused the stream… The connection limit may be reached.” usually means the account is playing somewhere else.
  • A stream that keeps closing every few seconds on a one-connection account is typically another viewer on the same login.

Slot Lag

Many panels keep counting a closed stream for a few seconds. When a channel or title is refused (or answered empty) before it started, Tachyon quietly asks again a few times over about 8.5 s, still showing Connecting…, and only then shows the connection-limit message. That is why a quick zap on a busy one-connection account can take a few seconds.

Buffering, Start-Up and Self-Healing

How streams start smoothly, ride out bad networks and come back after a drop.

Live TV

  • Playback starts on a clean buffer instead of the first frames, so there are no jumps at the start, and after a dip it refills properly instead of stuttering.
  • A watchdog looks for stalls, frozen pictures, buffer holes, drift and dropped connections. It recovers step by step: a nudge, a jump to the live edge, a reconnect with growing pauses, then the channel’s other format.
  • After the network comes back, playback resumes within about 3 seconds.
  • Some providers send 40–75 s of stored stream when a channel opens; playback then simply runs that far behind live. Tachyon doesn’t jump ahead and lose it.
  • A channel that is slow to start may take up to half a minute the first time; Tachyon says so and waits.
  • When the provider’s picture breaks up but the sound goes on, Tachyon keeps the sound and says so, instead of reconnecting over and over.
  • Desktop Slow Provider: Tachyon measures how fast a channel arrives against real time. When it stays clearly below (under about 92 % for some 20 seconds), a Slow Provider card says by how much (“about 61 % of real time”). No player can play such a channel without pauses, so Tachyon makes them fewer and longer instead of stopping every few seconds: each refill waits for more (from 8 s, growing to 30 s), without jumping to the live edge. The start of a channel is never delayed by it, and the card goes once the channel arrives in real time again. Another source of the channel may be fine: the card offers it when the channel has one.
  • A channel that stops sending and is then refused (HTTP 403) is said to be possibly off the air, not blamed on your connection limit; Tachyon keeps trying gently.
  • When the provider ends a channel’s connection while it plays, Tachyon asks for it again on the same one connection while the buffer keeps playing, so the picture carries on without a reconnect message.
  • Buffering and reconnecting show just a ring in the middle of the picture; anything that needs words comes in a slim banner at the top, and goes once playback is back.
  • Some channels’ sound runs on a broken clock. Tachyon times that sound from its own samples and keeps it with the picture, also after parts of the stream went missing.
  • After a stall, the cushion behind live grows a little, so the next hiccup is absorbed.

Movies and Episodes

  • When the provider sends a title slower than it plays, the player says by how much (“The provider sends this title at 1.8 Mb/s; it plays at 3.2 Mb/s…”).
  • Buffer Ahead then fills enough, once, to watch to the end without stops, with progress and a countdown; Play Now starts at once instead.
  • Deeper Buffer for Movies & Series makes each refill longer, so there are fewer, shorter breaks.
  • When a provider cuts the connection mid-file, the title picks up from the same byte at once, and each title’s index is kept on the device (Movie Index Cache) so reopening is instant. Desktop Tachyon also reads ahead up to 160 MB on the one connection.
  • Android The connection speed is measured continuously while a title loads. A fast connection buffers further ahead (up to 10 minutes, within the device’s memory), so a later slow patch is ridden out. With the controls up, a line under the stream tags shows what arrives and how much is buffered.
  • Desktop With Xtream, resting on a title’s page for a moment quietly fetches its index while the connection is free, so Play starts sooner.
One connection can only go as fast as the provider sends it. If a title is consistently slower than its bitrate, it’s the provider’s server or CDN, and Buffer Ahead is the way through.

Common Problems

No Sound on Some Channels

  • Android Turn off Audio Passthrough (Settings → Playback → Video Player) if your TV or receiver doesn’t take Dolby or DTS.

  • Android Open the player’s menu and set Decoder to Prefer Software for that channel.
  • Desktop Picture Repair starts by itself; press R to refresh if it doesn’t.
  • Try another version of the channel (4K / FHD / HD / SD).

  • All programmes early or late: set Guide Time Offset in the service’s Advanced options (or per guide under TV Guides).

Catch-Up Doesn’t Play

  • Leave Archive Type on Auto unless your provider tells you otherwise; Xtream Codes M3U links use the Xtream Codes type.

Empty Library or “Couldn’t Load Your Library”

  • Home shows the reason per service, with Refresh and Manage buttons.
  • Check the subscription hasn’t expired (the service card shows the expiry date), then refresh.

“Subscription Expired” When You Play

  • Tachyon knows the service’s subscription has ended, so it asks before playing: Try Anyway checks the account again (renewed, and it plays), Edit Service opens its settings, and Cancel closes the question by itself after 10 seconds.

“Library May Be Out of Date” in the Player Android

  • The provider no longer has the link a channel or title was saved with. Refresh Library leaves the player and updates that service’s library; Continue (by itself after a few seconds) carries on.

Movies and Episodes (VOD)

File layout and server behaviour that make titles start fast and seek instantly.

  • MP4 with the index first (faststart, moov before mdat): the player can start after the first few hundred KB. With moov at the end, every first open must fetch the end of the file first; on a slow CDN that can be seconds. Tachyon caches the index on desktop, but faststart is still best.
  • MKV with Cues (and a SeekHead pointing at them) for instant seeking.
  • Byte ranges: answer Range requests with 206 Partial Content and a correct Content-Length / Content-Range. Without ranges, seeking and resume can’t work properly.
  • Throughput above the bitrate: one connection must deliver at least the title’s bitrate (1.5× gives headroom). Tachyon shows viewers the measured rate when it’s lower.
  • Don’t cut connections mid-file. Tachyon resumes from the same byte, but every cut costs a round trip.
  • Redirects (302 to a CDN) are fine; keep the target stable while a title plays.
  • Clear errors: 404 for a missing title, 458 / 509 / 429 for a connection limit, never an empty 200 or a broken file.
  • Prefer H.264 / HEVC with AAC in MP4 or MKV. AVI, WMV and MPEG-PS go through the converter on desktop.

Connection Limits

How Tachyon’s one-connection rule meets a panel’s max_connections.

  • Tachyon holds one stream connection per account at most: active_cons should never go above 1 because of Tachyon on one device.
  • Zapping closes the old stream, waits about 0.3 s, then opens the next. Panels that keep counting a closed stream for a few seconds are tolerated: refusals (401, 403, 429, 458, 509) or an empty answer before the first data are re-asked a few times over ~8.5 s, never in parallel.
  • Release closed streams quickly. The faster the slot frees, the faster a viewer can zap.
  • Use 458, 509 or 429 for “too many connections”, and 404 for an off-air channel, rather than 403 for both: viewers then get the right explanation.
  • API calls (player_api.php, xmltv.php, get.php) are not stream connections. Tachyon never probes stream links with HEAD or test requests.
  • Default User-Agent: VLC/3.0.21 LibVLC/3.0.21; viewers can change it per service, and a playlist can set its own per channel.

A playlist file: a list of channel links with names, logos, groups and guide ids.
EPG / XMLTV
The electronic programme guide, usually an XMLTV file.
VOD
Video on demand: movies and episodes you start yourself.
Catch-Up
Programmes the provider keeps for a few days after they aired.
HLS / MPEG-TS / DASH
Ways of delivering a stream: playlists of short segments (HLS, DASH), or one continuous transport stream (MPEG-TS).
Codec
How picture or sound is compressed: H.264, HEVC, AAC, Dolby Digital…
Interlaced (1080i / 576i)
Broadcast pictures sent as two half-frames; they need deinterlacing on a computer screen.
Keyframe / GOP
A complete picture that playback can start from, and the group of pictures that follows it.
PTS / PCR
Timestamps inside a stream that keep picture and sound in step.
Direct Play / Transcode
Emby sending the file as it is, or converting it on the server for this device.
Passthrough
Sending Dolby / DTS sound untouched to a TV or receiver to decode.
Connection / Slot
One stream your plan allows at a time; a plan’s connection limit is its number of slots.
